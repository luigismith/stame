STAME
STAMEN HARMONICUM · TRIANDRIA

An open chord synth, grown on a handheld.

One hand holds a chord type (Dim, Min, Maj, Sus) and the extensions (6, m7, M7, 9), the other plays the root, and out comes a whole chord, voiced, with its bass. Performances (strum, arpeggio, pattern, harp), a Beat and an event looper let you build loops while you play: everything is played, nothing is programmed in steps. Stame does all of it on a Miyoo Flip, played with an Akai MPK mini IV and a fifteen-key macro pad. Then, with more keys, more knobs and more power than the original, a little more.

What it is

One finger, one chord. A whole band, if you keep going.

Stame grew out of a small synth with eight chord buttons on the left and one octave of keys on the right. Hold Min and press F♯: you get an F♯ minor chord, voiced in a window you choose with a dial, with a bass note under it. Add an extension (6, m7, M7, 9), let a performance play it for you (strum, arpeggio, pattern, harp), start a beat, record a loop, overdub. It is an instrument for people who hear chords before they can name them.

Stame reproduces that behaviour: the chord logic, the Secret Chords, the voicing windows, Key mode and transpose, the bass, the performances, the beat and the looper, the MIDI. It is described in a functional specification written from the public manual and from observation, before the first line of code; it holds no factory data of the original. The core is plain C11 — no heap, no I/O — so the same engine can move to dedicated hardware in phase 2.

Where the original has eight encoders and a 128×128 OLED, Stame has a keyboard, a macro pad and a 640×480 colour screen. The rule is simple: everything the original does, Stame does, and that behaviour is never altered. Then, because there are more keys, more knobs and more CPU, it does a little more — a sampler, sampled drum kits, a knob Lock, a view key.

The name, the greenhouse, the botanist

In a flower the stamen carries the pollen and the pistil receives it: Stame is the part that gives.

The rest of the identity comes from a greenhouse of the 1970s, the years of The Secret Life of Plants and of Mort Garson's Plantasia, Moog music «for plants and the people who love them». Stame is the synth that greenhouse would have had: a wooden console with a display that lights up like a slide under a microscope.

And from Linnaeus, who in 1735 classified plants by counting their stamens: one, Monandria; two, Diandria; three, Triandria. Stame keeps the rule: one note is one stamen. A triad is Triandria, a seventh chord Tetrandria, and the boot screen reads Stamen harmonicum, class Triandria. The chord name is set in dots, like a vacuum fluorescent display.

The rig

Three devices, one instrument.

Left hand on the pads, right hand on the keys, the macro pad a palm away, the Flip above it with the screen facing you.

Akai MPK mini IV

The eight pads are the original's chord buttons (Dim, Min, Maj, Sus / 6, m7, M7, 9); the keys give the root; the eight knobs turn Sound, Perform, FX, Key, Bass, Loop, BPM and Volume; the transport is the looper.

Macro pad

Fifteen keys and three detented, pushable knobs. The keys do what the original lacks: Hold, tap tempo, metronome, fill, four scenes, Save Loop, drums on the keys; the big knob is the Chord Voicing dial. T13 is the Drop, T14 the View, T15 the Page.

Miyoo Flip

The brain and the display: a 640×480 screen on a hinge, a Cortex-A55 running the synth at 48 kHz. Its own controls always work: the buttons move a focus and open the controls page and the menu, the left stick bends and modulates, the right one moves the two voicing windows, L2 taps the tempo and R2 holds a fill.

In the wooden console they sit like this: the MPK on the left, the pad in its cradle in line with the keys, the Flip open above the pad, so the screen never covers the keys. The rendering is further down, in the Case section.

The panel

A calm slide of fluorescent glass.

While you play, the screen shows four things: the chord, the tempo, the keyboard and the control you just touched. Everything else arrives when you ask for it.

How it sounds

Three synth models, a sampler, forty drum kits, sixteen voices.

Two engines, Sound and Bass, share a pool of sixteen voices at 48 kHz, with two FX slots each, a delay and a reverb. Everything is 32-bit float C11 that also compiles for a Cortex-M7.

Model

VA

Four band-limited oscillators, noise, a state-variable filter, four envelopes, four LFOs and an eight-slot modulation matrix.

Model

FM

Four operators, eight algorithms, feedback on the fourth operator: bells, organs, plucks and the glassy stuff.

Model

Reed Piano

A physical model of a 1960s reed electric piano: a tine with three modes, a non-linear pickup, clank, hammer and key-off noises, tremolo.

Stame addition

SMP, the sampler

A fourth model that plays a bank of CC0 samples: Kawai grand and upright pianos, a TX81Z FM piano, vibraphone, marimba, glockenspiel, chimes, harp, kalimba, harpsichord, pipe organ, recorder, tenor sax. Loops found and faded at build time, read with Hermite interpolation.

Beat

Sixty patterns, forty kits, a live drum machine

Funk, Bossa, Motorik, Lounge, Shuffle, Halftime, Boom Bap, Disco, Samba, Ballad, Train, Chip, Techno, Dub, Acid, Industrial, Minimal, Electro, Trap and, since 19 October, forty-one more by genre: House, Garage, Jungle, Dubstep, Synthwave, Lo-Fi, Chiptune, Glitch, EBM, Rock, Swing Jazz, Afrobeat, Teental, Gamelan, Salsa, Reggaeton, Taiko, Maqsum, Flamenco… each with a fill. Four kits of real samples (Studio, Jazz, 808, Techno), the synthesized CR-78-style Chip, and a library of 35 synthesized kits made of oscillators, noise and filters, from 808 Boom and 909 House to Gated 80s, Iron Mill, Glitch Bits, 8-Bit, Brushes, Tabla Mist and Gamelan, picked with a knob or from their list. Drums are played, never programmed: keys or the MPK's bank B pads, a roll knob, an accent knob, a swing knob and one for drive and filter on the drum bus, and every hit lands in the loop on the grid. And they invent themselves: with DRUMS on, the pad's three knobs give density, groove (thirty of them) and variation to a two-bar loop that changes while it plays.

Performance

Strum, Slop, Arpeggiate, Pattern, Harp

The chord can be played for you: strummed over one or two octaves, loosened, arpeggiated, sequenced by one of the patterns, or cascaded like a harp. Lock keeps the performance when you change sound.

Demo

«Acacia», «Felce», «Pistillo», «Alga», «Cicuta», «Torba»

Leave it alone for twenty seconds and Stame plays six original pieces by itself, in turn: chords, bass, loop with overdubs, a drum loop played on the keys and one invented with the knobs, acid and dub techno, sound changes, sampled piano, vibraphone, kalimba, sax, organ and bells, a ritardando. Every gesture shows on the panel as it happens.

Status · October 2026

What works today, what is specified and not built yet.

Honest list. The showcase on the Flip is version 0.1: it plays, it loops, it has a demo; it is not the finished instrument.

Running on the Flip

  • Chords from pads and keys, extensions, Secret Chords, Chord and Bass Voicing, Split / Octave, Key mode, transpose, Play Style
  • The real synth: VA, FM, Reed Piano and the SMP sampler, 200 Stame sounds, 24 basses, FX and reverb
  • Performances: Strum, Strum 2 Octaves, Slop, Arpeggiate (1 and 2 octaves), Pattern, Harp, with Lock
  • Beat with sixty patterns and forty kits, sampled and synthesized, metronome in four metres, beat volume
  • Looper: REC with a count-in, four bars, overdub, pause, undo, clear, synced with the transport
  • Macro pad: Hold, tap tempo, metronome, fill, four scenes, Options, Save Loop, drums, Panic (held 1 s) and Reset (held 3 s), T13 Drop (held: beat, loop drums and bass fall silent and come back on the beat), T14 View, T15 Page; the two voicing knobs, beat and bass volume
  • One instrument: keyboard, pad and Flip together, each command in one place. If a device is missing the others keep their job. The Flip's buttons always work
  • The Flip's sticks and triggers: bend and modulation on the left stick, Chord and Bass Voicing on the right one, tap tempo and fill on L2 and R2
  • MPK mini IV profile in the DAW preset: pads, keys, knobs, SHIFT as the push, navigation knob with − and +, transport, bank B as drum pads
  • The panel: play page, controls page, six views, two themes, lists, platform menu with Panic, four demos

Specified, not built yet

  • Save / Save As / Delete of User Sounds; loading a private factory bank from the SD card
  • Waiting Room and loop length (Free, 1–16 bars), ten loop slots, soft and hard exit
  • The other Mod combinations
  • MIDI out to the DIN port of the MPK, MIDI clock, external control
  • Pocket Mode (playing with the Flip alone), Learn Pad, MPK status page
  • The case: draft 5 complete on paper (cutting sheets, printed parts, SketchUp model, assembly instructions), not cut yet; the heights still to check
  • Phase 2: the same core on dedicated hardware (Teensy 4.1 + Audio Shield, probably)
Try it

Build on a PC, install over SSH.

You need a Miyoo Flip with a spruceOS-based firmware, an Akai MPK mini IV in its DAW preset, and, optionally, the fifteen-key macro pad on a plain USB 2.0 hub (no Power Delivery) on the Flip's top USB-C port.

The build runs in Git Bash on Windows with Zig as the C compiler (zig cc, cross-compiling for aarch64 glibc 2.27) and the SDL2 headers; the core compiles with no dependency at all. The tests and the portability checks must pass before every commit: the portability target compiles the core as freestanding Cortex-M7 code, to keep phase 2 honest.

sh tools/build.sh test          # core unit tests on the PC
sh tools/build.sh portabilita   # allowed headers, poisoned symbols, Cortex-M7 trial build
sh tools/build.sh render        # offline renderer: PNG frames and WAV of the demo
python -I tools/campioni.py     # downloads the CC0 samples and builds the bank (once)
sh tools/build.sh pacchetto     # the app for the Flip in build/pacchetto/App/Stame
sh tools/deploy.sh              # copies it over SSH (the Flip's address in tools/flip.ip)

On the Flip, Stame appears under App › Musica. The sample bank is built on the PC from tools/campioni.py (it needs numpy and soundfile) and shipped next to the binary; without it the sampled sounds and kits stay silent, everything else plays.

Playing it, in three lines

Hold a pad, press a key. Top row Dim, Min, Maj, Sus; bottom row the extensions 6, m7, M7, 9. The key gives the root; the big knob of the pad moves the voicing window.

Turn a knob, the band answers. K1 Sound, K2 Perform, K3 FX, K4 Key, K5 Bass, K6 Loop, K7 BPM, K8 Volume; hold SHIFT and touch a knob to «push» it. Hold T13 for a drop: beat, loop drums and bass go quiet and come back on the next beat.

REC, play four bars, ⊕ to overdub. The transport buttons of the MPK are the looper; STOP/PLAY starts the beat; T11 turns the keys into drums and T10 puts the loop aside. T12 held for a second is Panic, three seconds is Reset.

The Italian manual and the one-page quick reference have all of it.
